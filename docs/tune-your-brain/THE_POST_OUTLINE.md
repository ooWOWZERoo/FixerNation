**How each story plays (existing five-step story engine, no new code):** Recognize, Impact, Consider and Choose, then a private reflection that is never saved or sent. Steps 1 to 3 give three options (one careful, two believable but incomplete); step 4 gives four (careful, also good, unsafe, ask a trusted adult). Feedback on every option explains the effect. The writing rules are the same ones a test enforces for Choice Quest and Team Pick: balanced option lengths (within 1.4 times; the careful answer the longest in at most 2 of the 4 judged steps), no dashes, fictional names, and a believable "unsafe" option rather than a cartoon one.

## The four stories

### 1. A rumor about a teacher (public posts)
**Situation:** a post on an anonymous school meme account claims that Mr. Bell, a teacher, "got fired for something awful." It is false. The comments are filling up, and a friend wants you to add "lol true."

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Notice that it is probably false and could really hurt a real person, even if it is posted as a joke | "It is anonymous, so nobody can be hurt"; "The account is always joking" |
| Impact | The rumor can spread fast, harm his job and his family, and get everyone who shared it in trouble | "Teachers do not read these accounts, so it will fade"; "He is an adult, so he can handle it" |
| Consider | Do not comment or share, and report the post to the platform | "Reply that it is false in the comments" (can spread it); "Wait to see if someone else reports it" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** report the post and do not comment. **Also good:** tell your friend privately that you will not add to it and why. **Unsafe:** add a comment like "heard he is leaving" to fit in. **Ask a trusted adult:** tell a counselor or teacher if the rumor keeps growing. | |
| Reflect | "What would you want people to do if a rumor about you were spreading?" | |

### 2. A group project partner and an AI tool (schoolwork)
**Situation:** your partner on a history project turns in their part, and you notice it reads exactly like text from an AI tool. Your teacher's policy says the tool may not write the work, and the grade is shared.

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Notice that your own grade, and your partner's, may be at risk, and that you may be wrong about where the text came from | "It is their part, so it is not your business"; "Everyone uses these tools now" |
| Impact | It could count as cheating for both of you and make your partner stop learning the topic | "Nothing will happen because the teacher cannot tell"; "Only your partner is affected" |
| Consider | Talk to your partner first, privately, without accusing them | "Quietly rewrite their part yourself"; "Say nothing and hope it is fine" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** ask your partner, in private, about the writing and suggest redoing it in their own words. **Also good:** offer to outline the section together so it comes from them. **Unsafe:** turn it in as it is and say nothing, or rewrite it and hide the problem. **Ask a trusted adult:** ask your teacher how to handle it if you are unsure of the policy. | |
| Reflect | "How would you want a teammate to tell you something hard?" | |

### 3. A giveaway that asks for a login (safety online)
**Situation:** a friend reposts a "free concert tickets" giveaway. To enter, you must share the post, tag five friends and "verify" with your school email and password. The account is new and has a lot of followers.

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Notice that asking for your password is a warning sign, and that your friend may have been fooled | "It has thousands of followers, so it must be real"; "Free tickets are worth a small risk" |
| Impact | Someone could take over your account, message your contacts and use your school email | "Worst case you lose a few followers"; "Only you would be affected" |
| Consider | Do not enter, and check whether the real artist or venue posted the giveaway | "Enter with a fake password"; "Just share it without entering" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** do not enter or share, and tell your friend why in a kind message. **Also good:** look up the venue's real account and show your friend that it is not listed. **Unsafe:** enter your real login to get the tickets. **Ask a trusted adult:** report the account to the platform and tell a parent or a school staff member if you already entered. | |
| Reflect | "What helps you slow down before you share something?" | |

### 4. A tagged photo and your digital footprint (your own posts)
**Situation:** a friend posts a photo from the class trip of you making a silly face and tags you in a public post. You are about to apply for a summer job and a scholarship. People are leaving jokes in the comments.

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Notice that the photo is public and can be found later, and that your friend probably meant it kindly | "It is just a joke, so it is fine"; "Your friend did it on purpose to embarrass you" |
| Impact | Employers and schools often look people up, and a public photo stays findable for a long time | "Nobody looks at social media for jobs"; "You can delete everything later and it disappears" |
| Consider | Ask your friend, privately, to take the tag off or take the post down | "Comment a joke back to look relaxed"; "Say nothing and hope people forget" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** message your friend, say you liked the day, and ask them to remove the post or the tag. **Also good:** remove the tag yourself and change your settings so you must approve tags. **Unsafe:** post an embarrassing photo of your friend in return. **Ask a trusted adult:** talk to a parent or counselor about privacy settings if the photo keeps spreading. | |
| Reflect | "What would you want your public profile to say about you in a few years?" | |

## Spread checks

- **Four different situations** (a public rumor, schoolwork, a scam, your own footprint), so the Advance band no longer repeats the group-chat story.
- **Each story has a different kind of careful move:** report and stay out of it, talk privately first, protect your account, ask and then adjust settings. "Tell an adult" is the fourth option, never the first.
- **The careful option is not always the longest.** The "also good" option is a real alternative with its own trade-off, as in the other story games.
- **No story puts the student alone on the hook:** each offers a trusted-adult option and treats friends' mistakes as human.
- **Reading level:** about grade 8 to 12, plain and concrete.
