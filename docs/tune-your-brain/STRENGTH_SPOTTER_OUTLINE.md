# Strength Spotter (grades 3-5): the 12-story outline, for your approval

Written 2026-11-01. Outline only: no story text is written until you approve or edit each line. Concept approved 2026-11-01 (`NEW_GAMES_FOR_GAPS.md`, proposal 2). Each run would play 5 of these 12 stories.

**Links (draft, yours to sign off):** NY SEL 1B Identity and agency (direct), 1C Goals and learning from mistakes (supporting). Portrait of a Graduate: Reflective and Future Focused (direct), Creative Innovator (supporting). This is the first game in the catalog for 1B, which has no coverage today.

**How it plays (same engine as Feelings Detective):** a short story about a child (narrated and shown). Step one: "What does Maya do?" The child picks what really happened from a short list (some things are in the story, one is not). Step two: "Which strengths could Maya be showing? You can pick more than one." Two or three strengths fit, one fits less well, and the gentle note on that one says why. Nothing is scored right or wrong. Then a private check about the child's own strengths, never logged, sent or saved.

**One small engine change is needed.** The scenario-choice engine has two fixed prompts about feelings ("Which feeling(s) could this be?", "I've picked my feeling(s)") and a feelings-only summary label. I would add optional pack-level text for those prompts and the summary label. When the text is absent the engine behaves exactly as today, so Feelings Detective does not change. A test will prove that.

**The private check (same privacy rule as Feelings Detective):**
- "Do you use a strength like this?" Yes / No / Not sure.
- "Where do you use it?" At school / At home / With friends / Somewhere else / Not sure.
- A closing line, for example "Everyone has strengths, and they can grow." The answers are never logged, sent or saved. Only that the step was reached.

**Writing rules a test will enforce:** 3 to 4 actions in step one (3 in the story, 1 not); 4 strengths in step two (2 or 3 plausible, 1 less fitting with a gentle note); no strength is ranked or called "best"; no dashes; fictional names, mixed; strengths span school and not-school life (humor, listening, caring for animals, cooking, building); a trusted-adult step is not needed, since nothing here is distressing.

**Spread checks:** no two stories share the same set of strengths. Strengths cover caring, thinking, doing and trying. The "less fitting" strength is not always the opposite of the story (a "Luck" and an "Oldest" are subtler than "Being mean"), so a child cannot pass by avoiding the obviously negative one. Names mix genders and backgrounds, and no story pairs a strength with a particular group of children.

**Reading level:** about grade 3 to 5 by the readability estimate used for the other Explore games; narrated aloud.
