# Say It Another Way (grades 6-8): the 12-item outline, for your approval

Written 2026-11-01. Outline only: no passage or message text is written until you approve or edit each line. Concept approved 2026-11-01 (`NEW_GAMES_FOR_GAPS.md`, proposal 4). Each run would draw 6 of these 12; the four drafts are shuffled every time.

**Links (draft, yours to sign off):** NY SEL 2C Communicating across differences (direct); 2D Resolving conflict and 1A Emotions and actions (supporting). Portrait of a Graduate: Effective Communicator (direct), Global Citizen (supporting).

**How each item plays (same engine as Headline, no new code):** a short situation (4 sentences) that tells you who the listener is and what they need; "Which message would this person hear best?" with four drafts; then "Which sentence best shows why?" The proving sentence is the one that tells you what the listener needs.

**Trap types for the three wrong drafts** (every item uses at least two different kinds, and no item repeats the previous item's pair):
- **Blunt:** true, but cold; skips the other person's feelings
- **Blame:** "you always", accuses or labels
- **Vague:** hints and hopes, never says what you need or offer
- **Buried:** so many excuses or apologies the point is lost
- **Fake peace:** agrees or says "fine" to dodge the issue
- **Pressure:** puts the person on the spot, or in public
- **Talking down:** over-simplified or too loud, treats the listener as less able

**Right-draft style** is varied on purpose so that "pick the longest" and "pick the softest" both fail: *direct* (short and clear), *warm* (names the feeling and the plan), *question-first* (asks before telling). Draft lengths stay within 1.4 times of each other, and the right draft is the longest in at most 4 of 12.
