# Peace Table (grades 3-5): four situations, for your approval

Written 2026-11-01. Outline only: no step text is written until you approve or edit each line. This would be the second direct game for NY SEL 2D Resolving conflict (the first is Group Chat Meltdown, grades 6-8), and the first at grades 3 to 5. Each run would play one of four stories.

**Links (draft, yours to sign off):** NY SEL 2D Resolving conflict (direct), 1A Emotions and actions (supporting), 2A Empathy (supporting), 2C Communicating across differences (supporting). Portrait of a Graduate: Global Citizen (supporting), Effective Communicator (supporting).

**The idea:** two children want different things, or each thinks the other is wrong. The game walks through a "peace table": say what happened and how you feel, listen to the other side, find a fair plan, and check that it works. Nobody in the stories is a bully, and the best plan is rarely "win."

**How each story plays (existing five-step story engine, no new code):** the five steps become a peace-table cycle:

| Step (engine key) | In this game |
|---|---|
| Recognize | **Say what happened and how you feel** |
| Impact | **Listen:** what does the other child want, and why? |
| Consider | **Ideas:** three ways to find a fair plan |
| Choose | **Try it:** what do you say or do now? |
| Reflect | **Private reflection** (never saved or sent) |

Steps 1 to 3 give three options (one careful, two believable but incomplete); step 4 gives four (careful, also good, unsafe, ask a trusted adult). Feedback on every option explains the effect. The writing rules are the ones a test enforces for the other story games: balanced option lengths (within 1.4 times; the careful answer the longest in at most 2 of the 4 judged steps), no dashes, fictional names, a believable "unsafe" option (grabbing, yelling, getting even, taking sides), and nothing graphic.

## The four stories

### 1. Two kids want the same turn
**Situation:** Mia and Ben both want the last turn on the classroom computer, and the bell for lunch is about to ring. Each says "I was here first."

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Name what happened and how you feel: "I am frustrated, and I think I was next" | "It is just a computer, so you should not care"; "Say that Ben always does this" |
| Impact | Listen to Ben's side, and notice that he also wants a turn and is also hurried | "He is just being greedy"; "Whoever talks loudest gets it" |
| Consider | Share the turn, flip a coin, or split the time, so that both get something | "Let the teacher decide, without trying first"; "Take the computer while he is not looking" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** suggest taking turns, five minutes each, and say it calmly. **Also good:** suggest a coin flip and agree to accept it. **Unsafe:** grab the mouse and say it is yours. **Ask a trusted adult:** ask the teacher to help you decide if you cannot agree. | |
| Reflect | "What helps you stay calm when you want something badly?" | |

### 2. A borrowed thing gets broken
**Situation:** Priya borrows Amir's special pencil case, and the zipper breaks. Amir is upset. Priya feels bad and also thinks the zipper was already loose.

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Say what happened and that you feel bad, and say it was broken while you had it | "It was already broken, so it is not your fault"; "Hide it and say nothing" |
| Impact | Listen to Amir: it matters to him, and he feels sad and a little angry | "It is only a pencil case, so he should not be upset"; "He is overreacting" |
| Consider | Say sorry, offer to help fix it or replace it, and ask what would help | "Say sorry and walk away"; "Offer to give him something else instead" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** say sorry, say what you will do to help, and ask Amir what he wants. **Also good:** ask a grown-up to help fix the zipper together. **Unsafe:** say "it was already broken" and refuse to talk about it. **Ask a trusted adult:** ask a teacher how to make it right if you are not sure. | |
| Reflect | "How do you want people to treat something you lent them?" | |

### 3. A hurtful comment and a misunderstanding
**Situation:** Zoe tells Leo, "You are not in our group today." Leo hears that he is not wanted. Zoe meant that the group already has four players for the game, and she had meant to include him next round.

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Say what you heard and how it felt, and check that you understood | "Say nothing and feel sad"; "Say something mean back" |
| Impact | Ask what Zoe meant, and notice that she may not have meant it to sting | "She meant to hurt you, so she must be mean"; "It does not matter what she meant" |
| Consider | Ask a calm question, such as "Did you mean that I am not included?", and listen to the answer | "Ask your friends to take your side"; "Avoid Zoe for the rest of the day" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** ask Zoe what she meant, and tell her how it sounded. **Also good:** ask if you can play next round and say you felt left out. **Unsafe:** tell other kids that Zoe is unfair, so that they take your side. **Ask a trusted adult:** tell a teacher if the hurt feeling does not go away. | |
| Reflect | "When has something that you said sounded different from what you meant?" | |

### 4. "That is not fair, you cheated"
**Situation:** in a playground game, Ana says the ball was in and Jin says it was out. Ana says Jin always cheats. Jin says Ana always wins by arguing.

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Say what you saw and that you are upset about the call, without calling the other person names | "Say that Jin is a cheater"; "Quit the game and say it is not fun anymore" |
| Impact | Listen to Jin: he saw it differently, and he also feels accused | "He is lying, so there is nothing to listen to"; "Whoever has more friends here is right" |
| Consider | Agree on a fair way to decide, such as a do-over or a rule for close calls | "Let the loudest player decide"; "Ask a bigger kid who was not there" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** suggest a do-over, and agree on a rule for close calls before you play. **Also good:** suggest asking a neutral player who watched the whole point. **Unsafe:** shout that Jin cheats and refuse to play. **Ask a trusted adult:** ask the recess helper to listen to both sides and help you decide. | |
| Reflect | "How do you want to be treated when somebody thinks you did something wrong?" | |

## Spread checks

- **Four different kinds of conflict:** a shared turn, a broken thing, a hurtful misunderstanding, and an argument about a call. In each, both children have a fair point.
- **Each story has a different careful move:** share the turn, own it and repair it, ask what was meant, and agree on a rule. "Ask a grown-up" is the fourth option, never the first.
- **No story makes a child the bad guy:** Ben, Amir, Zoe and Jin are all treated as having a reasonable side.
- **No story puts the student alone on the hook:** each offers a trusted-adult option.
- **Reading level:** about grade 3 to 5, narrated like the other Explore games.
