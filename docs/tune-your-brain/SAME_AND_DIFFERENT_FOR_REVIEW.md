# Same and Different (grades K-2): the 12 finished rounds, for your read

Written 2026-11-01 from the outline you approved. **Held: not committed or deployed until you have read it.** Each run plays 4 of these 12 rounds. The scene and its question are read aloud, the child taps one of three picture choices, and a spoken line explains whichever one they pick. The three kinds of round (same, different, kind choice) are four each. Like the other Discover games, it uses the browser voice for now.

**What the tests enforce:** three picture choices per round; a spoken line on every choice; the right choice is the longest label in at most 4 of 12 rounds; a wrong choice in a same or different round says why it is not the answer; scenes read as one short passage that ends with the question; no dashes; fictional names.

**Please check:** rounds 4 (a home language), 6 (a wheelchair) and 8 (different families), the spoken "why not" lines on the wrong choices, and the closing line of each round.

## 1

**Read aloud:** Ana loves soccer, and she has a dog. Ben loves drawing, and he has a dog too. What is the same about Ana and Ben?

- **RIGHT:** 🐕 A dog
  - *Spoken:* Yes! Ana and Ben both have a dog.
- ⚽ Soccer
  - *Spoken:* Ana loves soccer, but Ben loves drawing. Look for something they both have.
- 🎨 Drawing
  - *Spoken:* Ben loves drawing, but Ana loves soccer. Look for something they both have.

**Spoken after any answer:** Friends are the same in some ways and different in others. Both are okay.

## 2

**Read aloud:** Mia wears glasses to help her see. Leo does not wear glasses. They both like the color red, and they are in the same class. What is different about Mia and Leo?

- **RIGHT:** 👓 Glasses
  - *Spoken:* Yes! Mia wears glasses and Leo does not.
- ❤️ Red
  - *Spoken:* They both like red, so that is the same. Look for something that is not the same.
- 🏫 Same class
  - *Spoken:* They are in the same class, so that is the same too.

**Spoken after any answer:** Glasses help Mia see. Being different is okay, and friends can still play together.

## 4

**Read aloud:** Sam is new in the class. At home, Sam speaks Spanish. Ana wants to play with him. What could Ana do?

- **RIGHT:** 🗣️ Ask for a word
  - *Spoken:* Yes! Asking Sam to teach you a word is kind, and you learn something too.
- ⏳ Wait and see
  - *Spoken:* If you wait, Sam plays alone. You can play together right now.
- 🙈 Play without him
  - *Spoken:* That leaves Sam out. Asking him to join is kinder.

**Spoken after any answer:** You can play with a friend who speaks a different language, and you can learn words from each other.

## 6

**Spoken after any answer:** Games can change so that everyone can play. Everybody belongs.

## 9

**Read aloud:** Ben is quiet and likes calm games. Ana likes loud, running games. Ana wants to play with Ben. What could Ana do?

**Spoken after any answer:** Friends can try a game one of them likes, and then a game the other one likes.

## 10

## 12

**Read aloud:** Maya wants to play with blocks. Dev wants to play tag. They both want to play together. What is a kind way to decide?

- **RIGHT:** 🔄 Take turns
  - *Spoken:* Yes! Taking turns means both friends get a game they like.
- 👆 Pick your favorite
  - *Spoken:* Then Dev does not get a turn. Taking turns is fairer.
- 🛑 Stop playing
  - *Spoken:* Then nobody gets to play. Taking turns is better.

**Spoken after any answer:** Taking turns is a kind way to play when friends like different things.
