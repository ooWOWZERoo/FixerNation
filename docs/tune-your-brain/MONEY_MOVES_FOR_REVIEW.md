# Money Moves: the reworked game, for your read (generated from the page)

Reworked 2026-11-01 per the approved recommendations. **Please read the 14 events and the five endings.** Nothing deploys until you say so. Fictional person and amounts; no financial advice. Every number below was checked: 25 percent off $32 is $24; 25 percent off $40 is $30, and $8 off $40 is $32; 8 percent tax on $25 is $2, so $27; a 10 percent match on $20 is $2, so $22, and on $10 is $1, so $11; 30 percent off $20 is $14; school lunch is $4 or $2 a day, so packing all five days saves $10 and three days saves $6; borrowing $20 with 10 percent extra means paying back $22, and $10 means $11.

**How a run works:** each run draws 6 of the 14 events in their written order and shuffles the choices. You start with $20 in your bike fund and $20 to spend. The goal is to reach $60 in the bike fund while keeping at least $10 to spend. A choice you cannot afford shows greyed out ("not enough spending money"); every event keeps one free choice, so a run can never get stuck. A test plays 4,000 random runs and confirms no counter ever goes below zero and all five endings can happen.

**What it teaches:** the 10 percent rule, comparing a percent discount with a dollar discount, sales tax, a savings match, interest on a loan, needs versus wants, and that one goal can be reached in more than one way. SEL link: setting and working toward a goal and learning from the result (NY SEL 1C); Portrait of a Graduate: Reflective and Future Focused, with Academically Prepared for the percent maths.

## The five endings (all kind; none says you failed)

- Bike fund $62, spending $14: You reached your goal and still have money to enjoy. You finished with $62 in your bike fund and $14 to spend. There is more than one way to get there, and you found one.
- Bike fund $62, spending $3: You reached your goal, and spending money got tight along the way. You finished with $62 in your bike fund and $3 to spend. That is a real tradeoff, and some people would choose it on purpose.
- Bike fund $45, spending $12: You are close to your goal. You finished with $45 in your bike fund and $12 to spend. A little more saving next season would get you there.
- Bike fund $25, spending $40: You kept plenty to spend and built a smaller bike fund. You finished with $25 in your bike fund and $40 to spend. If the bike matters most, try moving a bit more to savings next time.
- Bike fund $20, spending $8: You are off to a slow start, and that is normal for a first season. You finished with $20 in your bike fund and $8 to spend. Try a different mix and see how it changes.
