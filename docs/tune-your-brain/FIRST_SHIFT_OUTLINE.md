## The four stories

### 1. A coworker breaks something and asks you to cover (the existing story, rebalanced)
**Situation:** it is your second week at a new part-time job. Your coworker Jordan quietly breaks a piece of equipment and asks you to tell the manager you saw nothing, or that it was already broken before your shift.

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Notice that Jordan is asking you to give false information to a manager, which is bigger than a small favor | "It is just a small favor between coworkers"; "It is Jordan's problem, not yours" |
| Impact | The manager could decide on wrong information, and your own credibility could suffer if the truth comes out | "Nothing happens, because equipment breaks all the time"; "Jordan will thank you and it will be forgotten" |
| Consider | Talk to Jordan about telling the manager together, or decide what you will say if asked | "Avoid the manager for the rest of the shift"; "Tell Jordan you will say nothing unless someone asks you directly" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** tell Jordan that you cannot lie, and offer to be there if Jordan tells the manager. **Also good:** tell the manager honestly what you saw if asked, without adding detail. **Unsafe:** tell the manager it was already broken, as Jordan asked. **Ask a trusted adult:** talk to a family member or former teacher before your next shift. | |
| Reflect | "What would you want a coworker to do for you if you made a mistake at work?" | |

### 2. A shift swap promised to two people
**Situation:** you told your coworker Priya that you would cover her Saturday shift. Two days later, a friend invites you to a trip that same day, and you said yes before remembering. Priya is counting on you, and the schedule posts tonight.

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Notice that you made two promises that cannot both be kept, and that Priya has planned around yours | "It is only a shift swap, so it is not a big deal"; "Your friend asked second, so the trip does not matter" |
| Impact | Priya could be left without cover and in trouble with the manager, and your coworkers may stop trusting your word | "Someone else will cover it, so it will work out"; "Priya probably forgot, so no one will notice" |
| Consider | Tell Priya as early as you can, say sorry, and help find someone else | "Wait until the last minute in case something changes"; "Ask the friend to cancel, since you promised Priya first" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** tell Priya today, say sorry, and offer to find cover or trade another shift. **Also good:** keep your promise to Priya and tell your friend you cannot go. **Unsafe:** stay silent and skip the shift. **Ask a trusted adult:** ask a parent or the manager how to handle a conflict if you are unsure about the rules. | |
| Reflect | "What helps you keep track of promises when you say yes quickly?" | |

### 3. A rude customer and a new coworker
**Situation:** a customer is raising their voice at Sam, a coworker in his first week, over a mistake on an order. Sam looks shaken, and the line behind the customer is growing. You are standing a few steps away.

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Notice that Sam is upset and that the situation could get worse, and that you do not have to handle it alone | "Customers are always like this, so Sam will get used to it"; "It is not your table, so it is not your job" |
| Impact | Sam could lose confidence, the customer could get angrier, and other customers could be affected | "The customer will calm down by themselves"; "Sam has to learn to deal with it" |
| Consider | Stay calm, offer to help, and bring in a manager if the customer does not settle | "Argue with the customer so they stop"; "Step in and take over without checking with Sam" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** calmly offer to help the customer, and ask a manager to step in if the customer does not settle. **Also good:** check in with Sam quietly and offer to take the next customer for a few minutes. **Unsafe:** argue back or film it to post later. **Ask a trusted adult:** tell the manager afterward and ask what to do if it happens again. | |
| Reflect | "What helps you stay calm when someone around you is upset?" | |

### 4. Pay in cash with no paperwork
**Situation:** it is the end of the month, and your manager says he will pay your last two weeks in cash and skip the paperwork, "so you do not lose money to taxes." Several coworkers say it is normal. You are not sure what it means for you.

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize | Notice that you are not sure what it means, and that being paid without a record can affect you later | "Everyone does it, so it must be fine"; "More money in your pocket is simply better" |
| Impact | You could lose pay records you need for taxes, benefits or a future job, and be left without protection if there is a problem | "Nothing will happen, since no one checks"; "It is the manager's business, not yours" |
| Consider | Ask questions calmly and learn what the rules are before you agree | "Accept it today and sort it out later"; "Refuse angrily and confront the manager" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** politely ask for your regular paycheck and say you want it on the record. **Also good:** ask the manager to explain, then check with a parent or your school's work-permit office before you decide. **Unsafe:** take the cash and agree to keep it secret. **Ask a trusted adult:** talk to a parent, a guardian or a school counselor about what to do next. | |
| Reflect | "Who could you ask when you are not sure that something at work is okay?" | |

## Spread checks

- **Four different situations:** integrity, keeping your word, a tense moment with another person, and your own rights at work. All four are about a first job, which no other Advance game covers.
- **Each story has a different kind of careful move:** talk first and own what you saw, tell the person early and offer a fix, stay calm and bring in the manager, ask politely and get the facts. "Tell an adult" is the fourth option, never the first.
- **The careful option is not always the longest.** The "also good" option is a real alternative with its own trade-off.
- **No story puts the student alone on the hook:** each offers a trusted-adult option, and story 4 points to a parent or school office, not to a confrontation.
- **Reading level:** about grade 8 to 12, plain and concrete.
