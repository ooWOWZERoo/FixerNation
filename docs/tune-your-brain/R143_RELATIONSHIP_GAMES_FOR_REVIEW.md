# Release 143: ten Relationship Skills and Social Awareness games, for your read and ear test

Written 2026-11-06 by Claude. **Built, tested and played in a browser, committed locally but not pushed or deployed.** These ten games fill the two SEL focuses that were thinnest in every band. Content is data in `server/scripts/core-games-relationships.js`; this document is generated from it. **SEL wording: please read all of it.** The two Discover games are spoken; listen to them in `docs/tune-your-brain/r143-discover-clips-audition.html`.

| Band | Relationship Skills | Social Awareness |

Every band now has at least two games in both focuses. How each works: in the five reading games the child reads a short situation (4 or 5 sentences), picks the best answer from three, then taps the sentence that shows why (the same "answer, then prove it" shape as Headline and Say It Another Way). 10 situations each, 5 per run, choices shuffled. The wrong answers are written about as long as the right one (the right answer is the longest in 2 to 5 of 10 items, never more), and are real things people say or think, not cartoons.

## 🙋 Asking Nicely (discover, relationship skills)

*Listen to what happens, then pick the kind words to say.*

1. You want to play with the blocks. A friend has them. What do you say?
   Right: May I play?. Others: Grab them, Give me those. Explanation: Asking nicely helps friends say yes.
2. You bumped into a friend by accident. What do you say?
   Right: I am sorry. Others: Say nothing, Run away. Explanation: Saying sorry shows you care.
3. A friend shares a snack with you. What do you say?
   Right: Thank you. Others: Ask for more, Say nothing. Explanation: Thank you tells a friend you noticed.
4. You cannot reach the book on the shelf. What do you say?
   Right: Can you help me?. Others: Cry loudly, Give up. Explanation: Asking for help is a good way to get it.
5. You want the red crayon. Your friend has it. What do you say?
   Right: Ask for a turn. Others: Take it, Yell. Explanation: Asking for a turn keeps things friendly.
6. A friend says no to a game. What do you say?
   Right: Okay, maybe later. Others: Say they are mean, Cry and shout. Explanation: You can hear no and stay kind.
7. You want to sit by a new friend. What do you say?
   Right: Can I sit here?. Others: Push in, Stare. Explanation: Asking first makes room for everyone.
8. You want a turn on the swing. Someone is on it. What do you say?
   Right: Can I go next?. Others: Pull them off, Glare at them. Explanation: Waiting and asking is fair.
9. You are asking for water. What polite word do you add?
   Right: Please. Others: Hurry, Now. Explanation: Please makes a request sound kind.
10. A friend looks sad. You want to sit with them. What do you say?
   Right: Sit with them. Others: Walk by, Tell them to stop. Explanation: Asking shows you care about their feelings.
11. You spilled your friend's juice. What do you say?
   Right: Say sorry and help. Others: It was not me, Laugh. Explanation: Saying sorry and helping fixes the problem.
12. A friend helped you tie your shoe. What do you say?
   Right: Thank you. Others: Say nothing, Roll your eyes. Explanation: Thank you makes helping feel good.
13. You did not hear your teacher. What do you say?
   Right: Ask again. Others: Say nothing, Guess loudly. Explanation: Asking again helps you learn.
14. A game has started. You want to join. What do you say?
   Right: Can I play too?. Others: Stop the game, Take the ball. Explanation: Asking to join keeps the game fun.

## 🧱 Team Tower (discover, relationship skills)

*Listen to a team problem, then pick what helps the team.*

1. You and a friend build a tower. You both want the next block. What helps?
   Right: Take turns. Others: Take it, Walk away. Explanation: Taking turns is fair to both of you.
2. Your team is cleaning up. Your friend is tired. What can you do?
   Right: Help them. Others: Leave them, Tell on them. Explanation: Helping makes the team stronger.
3. Your group needs one big drawing. How can you share the jobs?
   Right: Each do a part. Others: Nobody starts, One does it all. Explanation: Sharing jobs gets the work done.
4. A friend cannot lift the box alone. What do you do?
   Right: Lift it together. Others: Watch, Laugh. Explanation: Many hands make a heavy job easy.
5. Your friend has a different idea. What helps the team?
   Right: Listen to it. Others: Say it is bad, Do your own. Explanation: Listening helps a team find good ideas.
6. The puzzle is hard. What helps?
   Right: Work on it together. Others: Quit, Throw the pieces. Explanation: Working together makes hard puzzles easier.
7. Two friends both want to lead. What helps?
   Right: Take turns leading. Others: Argue, Both quit. Explanation: Turns let everyone lead.
8. A new friend wants to help build. What do you say?
   Right: Come and help. Others: No room, Ignore them. Explanation: Inviting helps a new friend feel welcome.
9. Your team finished first. Another team is still working. What is kind?
   Right: Offer to help. Others: Say they are slow, Laugh. Explanation: Helping others shows good teamwork.
10. Someone dropped the blocks. What does the team do?
   Right: Pick up together. Others: Blame them, Walk away. Explanation: A team fixes mistakes together.
11. Your friend is shy in the group. What helps?
   Right: Ask for their idea. Others: Skip them, Talk over them. Explanation: Asking makes room for quiet voices.
12. The team has one marker. Everyone wants it. What helps?
   Right: Pass it around. Others: Hide it, Hold it. Explanation: Passing it around is fair.
13. Your team won a game. What do you say?
   Right: We did it together. Others: I did it, You were bad. Explanation: A team wins together.
14. Your team lost a game. What helps the team?
   Right: Say good game. Others: Blame someone, Throw the ball. Explanation: Good sports keep the team together.

## 🚀 Team Mission (explore, relationship skills)

*Read a group work problem, pick the best team move, then find the sentence that shows why.*

1. Four students are making a poster about frogs. Nia wants to draw and Omar wants to write. They keep arguing about who goes first. Twenty minutes are left.
   Q: What would help the group most?
   Right: Give each person a job to do.
   Others: Let the loudest person decide. | Wait for the teacher to fix it.
   Proof sentence: "Nia wants to draw and Omar wants to write."
   Explanation: Each person wants a different job. Splitting the work solves it.

2. Ben is quiet in group work. He has an idea but does not say it. The group is stuck on the next step. Mia sees Ben looking at the paper.
   Q: What could Mia do?
   Right: Ask Ben what he is thinking.
   Others: Pick the next step herself. | Tell Ben to talk louder.
   Proof sentence: "He has an idea but does not say it."
   Explanation: Ben has an idea. Asking him invites him in.

3. Two friends share one computer for a project. Sam types fast and does not let Zoe try. Zoe feels left out. She wants to help.
   Q: What should Zoe say?
   Right: Can we switch every five minutes?
   Others: You never let anyone have a turn. | Never mind, you can keep going.
   Proof sentence: "Sam types fast and does not let Zoe try."
   Explanation: Zoe needs a turn. A fair plan fixes it.

4. Our team cannot agree on a science fair topic. Half want plants and half want rockets. We could vote or find a topic with both. Time to choose is almost up.
   Q: Which choice is fair to everyone?
   Right: Vote, or combine both ideas.
   Others: Let the captain choose alone. | Pick the first idea anyone said.
   Proof sentence: "We could vote or find a topic with both."
   Explanation: Both groups want a say. A vote or a mix is fair.

5. Lena finished her part of the group map early. Jake is still working on his part and is stuck. Lena could start something new or help Jake. The map is due tomorrow.
   Q: What is the best team move?
   Right: Offer to help Jake with his part.
   Others: Start a new part of the map alone. | Tell the teacher Jake is too slow.
   Proof sentence: "Jake is still working on his part and is stuck."
   Explanation: Jake is stuck. A team helps a teammate who needs it.

6. Ava made a mistake on the group chart. Tom noticed and feels annoyed. Ava looks worried. The chart is due in ten minutes.
   Q: What should Tom say?
   Right: Let us fix it. I will help.
   Others: You always mess things up. | Nothing. I will fix it later.
   Proof sentence: "Ava looks worried."
   Explanation: Ava is worried. Kind help fixes it fast.

7. Five students are practicing a play. Dee keeps forgetting her lines. Two students sigh loudly. Dee looks down at the floor.
   Q: What would help the group?
   Right: Offer to practice lines with Dee.
   Others: Give Dee's part to someone else. | Ignore it and keep rehearsing.
   Proof sentence: "Dee looks down at the floor."
   Explanation: Dee looks upset. Practicing together helps her.

8. Our group has to share the art supplies. Pat took all the markers and will not share. Other group members are waiting. Time is passing.
   Q: What could someone say?
   Right: Can I use some markers soon?
   Others: Give me those markers right now. | I will just sit here and wait.
   Proof sentence: "Pat took all the markers and will not share."
   Explanation: Pat has all the markers. A polite ask can fix it.

9. Rosa has a different idea from the rest of her group. They all want a poster, but Rosa wants a video. Rosa feels nobody listened. The group can only do one.
   Q: What helps the group decide?
   Right: Hear her idea, then choose together.
   Others: Do the poster since most want it. | Let Rosa make her own video alone.
   Proof sentence: "Rosa feels nobody listened."
   Explanation: Rosa feels unheard. Listening first makes the choice fair.

10. Your team won the class game. The other team looks upset. One of your teammates starts to cheer loudly. The teacher is watching.
   Q: What is a good teamwork move?
   Right: Say good game to the other team.
   Others: Cheer louder with your teammate. | Tell them they should practice more.
   Proof sentence: "The other team looks upset."
   Explanation: The other team looks upset. Kind words keep it friendly.

## 📱 Other Side of the Screen (challenge, social awareness)

*Read an online moment, work out how the other person feels, then find the proof.*

1. Cam posts a video of his first guitar song. Some comments say it is awful and cringe. Cam deletes the video and stops answering messages. A friend notices he has gone quiet at school.
   Q: What is Cam most likely feeling?
   Right: Hurt and embarrassed by the comments.
   Others: Pleased that the video got attention. | Curious about what to post next.
   Proof sentence: "Cam deletes the video and stops answering messages."
   Explanation: Deleting the video and going quiet shows the comments hurt.

2. Rita sends a joke to the group chat at midnight. Nobody replies for an hour. She wonders if everyone is mad at her. In the morning she learns they were all asleep.
   Q: What does this show about messages?
   Right: Silence does not always mean anger.
   Others: Silence always means someone is upset. | Late night jokes are always a mistake.
   Proof sentence: "In the morning she learns they were all asleep."
   Explanation: Everyone was asleep, not angry. Silence can be only silence.

3. Theo writes "fine" in reply to a question about plans. His friend Ana reads it as annoyed. Theo meant he was happy to go along. They argue before they talk it through.

   Others: Ana should have ignored the message. | Theo should have written much more.
   Proof sentence: "His friend Ana reads it as annoyed."
   Explanation: Ana read the word as annoyed. Without a voice, tone is easy to miss.

4. A classmate shares a photo of a messy room as a joke. People in the comments laugh. The photo shows Lena's home, taken without asking. Lena sees it and does not go to school the next day.

## 🔧 Repair It (advance, relationship skills)

*Read what went wrong, then pick the message that owns it and repairs trust.*

1. Jules snapped at a coworker during a busy shift. The coworker stayed quiet and avoided Jules for the rest of the day. Jules wants to fix things before the next shift. The coworker has said they value honesty.
   Q: Which message would repair the relationship?
   Right: I was short with you yesterday, and that was not fair. I am sorry.
   Others: Sorry if you took it the wrong way. I was just having a really bad day at work. | I should not have to apologize, since that whole shift was chaos for everyone.
   Proof sentence: "The coworker has said they value honesty."
   Explanation: The coworker values honesty. A clear apology owns the harm.

2. Amara missed her friend Beck's birthday dinner without telling him. He found out from social media that she was at a party. He has not texted her in a week. She misses the friendship.
   Q: Which message would repair the relationship?
   Right: I should have told you I could not come, and I left you guessing. I am sorry.
   Others: I know you are mad, but it was just one dinner and you took it too far. | Hey. What is up with you lately? I miss you, so text me back sometime soon.
   Proof sentence: "He found out from social media that she was at a party."
   Explanation: Beck learned the truth from a post. Owning that is the first step.

3. Kai gave a teammate harsh feedback in front of the whole group. The teammate went red and stopped contributing. Kai realizes the point was fair but the delivery was not. The project meeting is tomorrow.
   Q: Which message would repair the relationship?
   Right: My feedback was fair, but I should have said it privately. I am sorry.
   Others: I am not apologizing for being honest. Somebody had to say it out loud. | Sorry about that. Let us just pretend it never happened and move on.
   Proof sentence: "Kai realizes the point was fair but the delivery was not."
   Explanation: The point was fair and the delivery was not. The first message separates them.

4. Nadia shared a friend's secret with another friend. The secret got back to the first friend, Lou. Lou says she does not know if she can trust Nadia again. Nadia wants to take responsibility.
   Q: Which message would repair the relationship?

   Others: I only told one person, so it is not that serious. I honestly do not see the problem here. | I already said sorry once. Why are we still talking about this when it is over?
   Proof sentence: "Lou says she does not know if she can trust Nadia again."
   Explanation: Lou questions trust. The first message takes responsibility and makes a promise.

5. Marcus was late to three study sessions in a row. His study partner Elle felt her time was disrespected. Elle texted, "Never mind, I will study alone." Marcus wants to rebuild the plan.

   Proof sentence: "His study partner Elle felt her time was disrespected."
   Explanation: Elle felt disrespected. The first message names the cost and offers a plan.
