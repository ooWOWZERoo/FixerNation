## How each story would be built

Same five-step cycle the two games use today (recognize, impact, consider, choose, reflect), using the `variants` support from Phase 1, so the game picks one whole story per run. The existing story stays as story 1, so each game ends up with four.

Writing rules applied to every story (these come from the scorecard findings):
1. **Steps 1 to 3 get three options, not two**, and the wrong ones are believable ("plausible but incomplete"), never strawmen like "she's just lazy".
2. **Lengths are balanced.** Every option in a step is within about 15 percent of the others' length, and the best answer is sometimes the shortest.
3. **The final step has four options:** one responsible, one other defensible, one unsafe-but-believable, one "tell a trusted adult". The unsafe one is realistic, not melodramatic.
4. **Every option has feedback that explains the effect** on people, never just "right" or "wrong".
5. **Closing reflection is private** (never logged or sent), as today.
6. **Reading level:** short sentences, about 80 to 110 words per step, fictional names only, a mix of names.
7. Each story is checked against the audit script (rounds, length balance) before it ships.

Where a story touches a sensitive experience, a note says so for your review.

## Choice Quest: empathy and responsible decisions

Story 1 stays as is (Mia's unfinished part of the diorama). Three new stories:

**CQ2. The borrowed markers** (about owning a mistake)
- *Setup:* You borrowed Dev's brand-new marker set for a poster. When you open the case at home, one marker has been left uncapped and has dried out.
- *Recognize:* notice you feel worried and that Dev will find out either way, vs. "it's only one marker" vs. "maybe he won't notice".
- *Impact:* what returning it quietly changes for Dev, for trust, and for you.
- *Consider:* options range from saying nothing, to replacing it secretly, to telling Dev first.
- *Choose:* tell Dev before he finds it (responsible); offer to replace it and ask what he'd like (also defensible); return it and hope he never checks (unsafe, believable); ask a parent or teacher how to handle it (trusted adult).
- *Reflect:* "What is one thing you could say to someone when you have made a mistake with something that isn't yours?"
- *NY mapping (draft):* SEL 1C (own mistakes) direct, 3B direct, 2A supporting; PoG Global Citizen supporting.

**CQ3. The rules that keep changing** (about fairness and conflict)
- *Setup:* At recess, Ravi keeps changing the rules of tag so that he is never "it". Your friends are getting annoyed and one has started to quit.
- *Recognize:* notice what is actually happening (it feels unfair to several people) vs. "Ravi's just competitive" vs. "it's only a game".
- *Impact:* how this affects the game, the friendship and Ravi himself, who may not realise.
- *Consider:* quitting, calling him out in front of everyone, asking the group for fixed rules, asking a recess helper.
- *Choose:* suggest agreeing the rules before the next round, as a group (responsible); talk to Ravi privately about how it feels (also defensible); tell everyone Ravi is a cheater (unsafe, believable); ask a recess aide to help (trusted adult).
- *Reflect:* "What is one fair way to decide rules before a game starts?"
- *NY mapping (draft):* SEL 2D (prevent, manage and resolve conflicts) direct, 2C supporting, 3A supporting; PoG Effective Communicator supporting.

**CQ4. A secret that isn't yours** (about trust and respect)
- *Setup:* Your friend Lena tells you she is nervous about reading aloud tomorrow. At lunch another classmate asks you, "Is Lena scared of the reading?"
- *Recognize:* notice that this was told to you in trust and that someone is asking you to pass it on.
- *Impact:* what happens to Lena's trust, and to how Lena feels tomorrow, if it spreads.
- *Consider:* options that change the subject, give a vague answer, or tell the truth.
- *Choose:* say "That's Lena's to share," and offer to help Lena practise (responsible); tell the classmate you don't know (defensible, but discuss: is it honest?); share it "because it's not a big deal" (unsafe, believable); ask a teacher how to support Lena (trusted adult).
- *Reflect:* "What helps you know whether something is yours to share?"
- *NY mapping (draft):* SEL 2C (communicate with empathy) direct, 3A and 2A supporting; PoG Global Citizen and Effective Communicator supporting.
- *Sensitivity:* the "(defensible, but discuss)" option is the kind of line I would like you to read closely. It is honest-ish, and the feedback has to say why a flat "I don't know" still isn't quite right.

## Team Pick: self-management

Story 1 stays as is (picked last in gym). Three new stories:

**TP2. The missed catch** (about embarrassment and mistakes)
- *Setup:* In kickball your team loses by one run after you miss an easy catch. A few teammates groan. Your face feels hot.
- *Recognize:* name the feeling (embarrassed, maybe a little angry) vs. "I don't care" vs. "it was everyone's fault".
- *Impact:* what happens to the rest of recess if the feeling takes over, and to how teammates see you.
- *Consider:* calming moves, a quick word to the team, walking away.
- *Choose:* take a breath, then say "I missed that one, I'll get the next" (responsible); step aside for a minute and rejoin (also defensible); blame the sun or the ball (unsafe, believable); tell the recess aide how you feel (trusted adult).
- *Reflect:* "What can you tell yourself the next time you make a mistake in front of people?"
- *NY mapping (draft):* SEL 1A direct, 1C direct (learn from mistakes), 2D supporting; PoG Reflective and Future Focused direct.

**TP3. The spelling test** (about disappointment and goals)
- *Setup:* You studied all week, but your spelling test score is lower than you hoped. A friend next to you got a perfect score.
- *Recognize:* name the feelings (disappointed, maybe jealous) vs. "I'm just bad at spelling" vs. "tests don't matter anyway".
- *Impact:* what happens to how you study and feel if you decide you're just bad at it.
- *Consider:* look at which words were missed, ask for help, set a small goal, compare yourself to your friend.
- *Choose:* look at the missed words and pick three to practise tonight (responsible); ask your teacher how to study differently (also defensible, and uses an adult); decide spelling isn't for you and stop trying (unsafe, believable for self-talk); celebrate your friend and ask how they study (trusted help: peer).
- *Reflect:* "What is one small goal you could set after a result you didn't expect?"
- *NY mapping (draft):* SEL 1C direct (set goals, learn from mistakes), 1A supporting; PoG Reflective and Future Focused direct.
- *Sensitivity:* comparing yourself to a high-scoring friend can sting. The story is kind about it, but flagging it for you.

**TP4. The part in the play** (about jealousy and supporting a friend)
- *Setup:* The class play parts are announced. Your friend Zoe got the lead you wanted. You get a small part.
- *Recognize:* name the feeling (jealous, let down, and also a bit happy for Zoe) vs. "it's not fair" vs. "I'm fine".
- *Impact:* what the feeling does to the friendship and to rehearsals if you keep it all inside.
- *Consider:* talk to Zoe, talk to the teacher, pour effort into your own part.
- *Choose:* tell Zoe congratulations and do your best with your part (responsible); tell a trusted friend how you feel first, then talk to Zoe (also defensible); say the teacher picked her because she's the teacher's favourite (unsafe, believable); ask the teacher what to work on to earn a bigger part next time (trusted adult).
- *Reflect:* "What can you do with a feeling like jealousy so it doesn't hurt a friendship?"
- *NY mapping (draft):* SEL 1A direct, 2A direct, 2C supporting; PoG Reflective and Future Focused and Global Citizen supporting.
