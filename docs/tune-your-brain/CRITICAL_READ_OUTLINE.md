Written 2026-11-01. Outline only: no passage text is written until you approve or edit each line. The design is the one you approved in `READING_GAMES_DESIGN.md`, and the plan is the same as Headline's: 12 items, 6 drawn per run, the four conclusions shuffled every time.

**Links (draft, yours to sign off):** Portrait of a Graduate: Critical Thinker (direct), Global Citizen (supporting, as an informed reader of news and ads). No SEL link, and none is needed.

**How each item works (same engine as Headline and Say It Another Way):** a short passage (five to six sentences) with a source, a claim and a gap; "Which conclusion does the passage best support?" with four conclusions; then "Which sentence best supports your answer?" The proving sentence is the one that holds the key fact or the key limit.

**Trap types** for the three wrong conclusions (every item uses at least two different kinds, and no item repeats the previous item's pair):
- **Naive:** takes the claim at face value
- **Cynical:** throws the claim out because of a flaw that does not make it worthless
- **Scope:** stretches the result to people or places that were not measured
- **Number:** misreads a number (a count and a share, points and percent, an average)
- **Cause:** says one thing caused another when the passage only shows they go together
- **Source:** gets the role of who wrote it, paid for it or was quoted wrong
- **Missing piece:** true, but leaves out the fact that matters most

**Right-conclusion style** is varied on purpose so that "pick the careful one" and "pick the confident one" both fail: *bold* (the evidence supports a firm conclusion), *plain* (it says what the numbers say), or *hedged* (the evidence is real but limited). Hedged is right in only 4 of 12 items.
