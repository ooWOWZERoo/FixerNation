# Values Compass (grades 6-8): the 12-story outline, for your approval

Written 2026-11-01. Outline only: no story text is written until you approve or edit each line. This would be the second direct game for NY SEL 1B Identity and agency (the first is Strength Spotter, grades 3-5). Each run would play 5 of these 12 stories.

**Links (draft, yours to sign off):** NY SEL 1B Identity and agency (direct), 1C Goals and learning from mistakes (supporting). Portrait of a Graduate: Reflective and Future Focused (direct), Critical Thinker (supporting).

**How it plays (the same engine as Strength Spotter and Feelings Detective, no new code):** a short story about a student (narrated and shown). Step one: "What do you notice in this story?" The student picks what really happens (three things in the story, one not). Step two: "Which values could this show? You can pick more than one." Three values fit and one fits less well, and the gentle note on that one says why. Nothing is scored right or wrong, and no value is ranked above another. Then a private check: "Does this matter to you?" Yes / A little / Not sure, then "Where does it show up in your life?" At school / At home / With friends / Somewhere else / Not sure. The answers are never logged, sent or saved; only that the step was reached.

**The engine needs nothing new:** Strength Spotter's optional wording for the prompts is already in the engine, so this game supplies its own ("Which values could this show?", "I've picked my values", "Times you saw more than one value").

**What is different from Strength Spotter:** at grades 3 to 5 the question is "what am I good at?" At grades 6 to 8 it is "what do I care about, and what do I do when it costs something?" The stories include choices that cost the character something (friends, a grade, comfort), and the game never says which choice was right. Agency matters too: two stories show a student deciding for themselves, including when a friend wants to decide for themselves.

**Writing rules a test will enforce:** 4 cues (3 in the story, 1 not); 4 values (3 that fit, 1 that fits less, with a gentle note); no two stories share the same set of values; no dashes; fictional names, mixed; no politics, religion or contested issues; the private check keeps its order and is never logged.

**Spread checks:** no two stories share a set of three values; the less-fitting value is subtle (Popularity, Winning, Natural talent) and never a plain bad trait; stories 6 and 12 give agency to the student and to the friend; names mix genders and backgrounds, and no story pairs a value with a group of students.

**Reading level:** about grade 6 to 8, narrated.
