# Perspective Swap (grades 9-12): four situations, for your approval

Written 2026-11-01. Outline only: no step text is written until you approve or edit each line. Concept approved 2026-11-01 (`NEW_GAMES_FOR_GAPS.md`, proposal 5). Each run would play one of four stories.

**Links (draft, yours to sign off):** NY SEL 2B Similarities and differences (direct), 2C Communicating across differences (direct), 2A Empathy (supporting), 2D Resolving conflict (supporting). Portrait of a Graduate: Global Citizen (direct), Critical Thinker (supporting). After this, 2B and 2C each have two direct games in the Advance and younger bands, which is the point.

**What the game is:** a group has a plan that works for most people and not for one teammate. You see the situation from two teammates in turn, then choose how the group can adjust so everyone can take part. Nobody in these stories is a villain, and no group is the problem. The differences come from faith, communication style, money and access, always written without naming a real group.

**How each story plays (existing five-step story engine, no new code):** the five steps become a perspective cycle:

| Step (engine key) | In this game |
|---|---|
| Recognize | **See it from the first teammate's side:** what is happening for them? |
| Impact | **See it from the second teammate's side:** what is happening for them? |
| Consider | **Ideas:** three ways the group could adjust |
| Choose | **Act:** what do you do or say now? |
| Reflect | **Private reflection** (never saved or sent) |

Steps 1 to 3 give three options (one careful, two believable but incomplete); step 4 gives four (careful, also good, unsafe, ask a trusted adult). Feedback on every option explains the effect. The writing rules are the ones a test enforces for the other story games: balanced option lengths (within 1.4 times; the careful answer the longest in at most 2 of the 4 judged steps), no dashes, fictional names, a believable "unsafe" option. In this game "unsafe" means a move that makes one person carry the whole cost of the difference, or that treats a difference as a problem, never something graphic.

## The four stories

### 1. A deadline on a day that matters to a teammate's family
**Situation:** your group sets a final practice for Saturday morning before a Monday presentation. Amira says that day is a family celebration of a holiday that matters a great deal to them, and she will be away until Sunday night. Two others say the group should keep Saturday because it is the only day everyone else is free.

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize (Amira's side) | Notice that the day matters to her family and that she may feel she has to choose between her family and the group | "It is only one practice, so it should not be hard for her"; "She should have said so earlier" |
| Impact (the group's side) | Notice that the others are worried about time and want the presentation to go well, which is a fair worry | "The others are being selfish"; "Their schedule is the only one that counts" |
| Consider | Look for a time that works for all, such as Sunday night or a call on Friday, before anyone gives something up | "Keep Saturday, since most people can come"; "Let Amira join by message and catch up later" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** propose a Friday or Sunday practice and ask everyone what works. **Also good:** offer to practice her part with her separately and share notes with the group. **Unsafe:** go ahead on Saturday and tell her to catch up on her own. **Ask a trusted adult:** ask your teacher if the date can move if no time works. | |
| Reflect | "When has a plan that worked for you been hard for someone else?" | |

### 2. A fast group discussion and a teammate who waits for a pause
**Situation:** your group's discussions move quickly, and people often talk over each other. Kenji, who grew up where interrupting is considered rude, waits for a pause that never comes. He has said very little in three meetings, and the group has started to say that he is "not into it."

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize (Kenji's side) | Notice that he may be waiting for his turn out of respect, and that being quiet is not the same as not caring | "He just does not care about the project"; "He is shy, so it is his problem to fix" |
| Impact (the group's side) | Notice that the group moves fast because they want to finish, and does not realize that it is shutting someone out | "The group is doing nothing wrong, because that is how meetings work"; "Everyone should speak up if they want to be heard" |
| Consider | Agree on a simple way to take turns, such as going around the table, and ask for ideas in writing too | "Ask Kenji to speak up more"; "Keep the meetings as they are and add a note later" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** suggest that the group go around the table for each topic, and say why it helps everyone. **Also good:** ask Kenji privately how he likes to share ideas and offer to bring them to the group. **Unsafe:** tell the group that he is not pulling his weight. **Ask a trusted adult:** ask the teacher or advisor for help setting up group norms. | |
| Reflect | "What helps you speak up, and what helps you listen?" | |

### 3. A trip that assumes everyone can pay
**Situation:** your group is planning a celebration dinner at a restaurant that costs $45 each. Lucia quietly says she cannot afford it this month and says she will skip. Two others think splitting the cost is only fair and that "everyone can manage $45."

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize (Lucia's side) | Notice that she may be embarrassed, and that she told the group out of honesty and trust | "She should just find the money"; "She is making a big deal of nothing" |
| Impact (the group's side) | Notice that the others may assume that everyone has the same budget, and not realize that it excludes someone | "Splitting equally is always fair"; "They are being unkind on purpose" |
| Consider | Look for a plan that costs less or lets people choose, without asking Lucia to explain her money | "Ask Lucia to explain why she cannot pay"; "Have the group chip in for her in front of everyone" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** suggest a lower-cost option for everyone, such as a potluck or a cheaper place. **Also good:** ask the group to set a spending limit before choosing a place. **Unsafe:** collect money from the group to cover her and announce it. **Ask a trusted adult:** ask the advisor whether the activity can have a budget. | |
| Reflect | "How can a group plan something fun that does not depend on how much money people have?" | |

### 4. A video with no captions and a teammate who is hard of hearing
**Situation:** your group is planning a video presentation. Marcus, who is hard of hearing, says that without captions he cannot follow the videos that the group plans to watch together for research. Two teammates say that captions would take too much time and that "he can just read the transcript later."

| Step | Careful option | Believable but incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize (Marcus's side) | Notice that he wants to take part in the research on equal terms, not afterward | "He can catch up from the transcript later"; "He should ask the teacher for a special arrangement" |
| Impact (the group's side) | Notice that the others are worried about time, and may not know how quick adding captions is | "Captions are a favor for one person"; "The group should not have to change for one teammate" |
| Consider | Look for videos that already have captions, or turn on automatic captions and check them together | "Skip the videos he cannot follow"; "Have him watch separately" |
| Choose (4) | **Careful:** pick videos with captions and turn on captions for the video the group makes. **Also good:** propose that the group watch together with captions on and share notes after. **Unsafe:** go ahead without captions and tell him to read the transcript. **Ask a trusted adult:** ask the teacher about caption tools the school provides. | |
| Reflect | "What small change could make something easier for more people than the one it was made for?" | |

## Spread checks

- **Four different kinds of difference:** faith and family, communication style, money, and access. None names a real group, and none is a problem to fix.
- **Each story has a different careful move:** find a time that works for all, change how the group takes turns, lower the cost for everyone, and make the tool work for everyone. "Ask an adult" is the fourth option, never the first.
- **Both sides are heard:** Steps 1 and 2 show each teammate's point of view in their own terms, and the group's worry is treated as fair.
- **No story puts the teammate with the difference alone on the hook:** the unsafe options are the ones that make that person carry the cost.
- **Reading level:** about grade 8 to 12, plain and concrete.
