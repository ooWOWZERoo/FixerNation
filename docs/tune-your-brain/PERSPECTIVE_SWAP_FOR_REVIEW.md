# Perspective Swap (grades 9-12): the four finished stories, for your read

Written 2026-11-01 from the outline you approved. **Held: not committed or deployed until you have read it.** Each run plays one of the four stories.

**What the tests enforce (the same rules as the other story games):** three options in steps 1 to 3 (one careful, two believable but incomplete) and four in step 4 (careful, also good, unsafe, ask a trusted adult); feedback on every option; option lengths within 1.4 times; the careful answer is the longest in at most 2 of the 4 steps; no dashes; fictional names; a private reflection that is never saved or sent.

**Please check:** the most delicate of the Advance games. Story 1 mentions a family holiday without naming a faith, story 3 mentions money, and story 4 mentions a teammate who is hard of hearing. No real faith, country or condition is named beyond that. The "unsafe" options are the ones that make the teammate carry the whole cost. Check the "from his or her side" lines in steps 1 and 2, and the feedback lines students read.

## Story 1: A deadline on a day that matters to a teammate's family

**1. Recognize (the first teammate's side).** Your group plans a final practice for Saturday morning before a Monday presentation. Amira says that Saturday is a family celebration of a holiday that matters a great deal to them, and she will be away until Sunday night. What do you notice from her side?

- (careful move) The day matters to her family, and she may feel that she has to choose between them and the group.
  - *That is the real weight of it. Seeing the choice she is facing helps you respond to her, not just to the schedule.*
- (believable but incomplete) It is only one practice, so it should not be hard for her to move her plans around.
  - *A single practice can matter a lot when the other side is a family occasion. What looks small to you may not be small to her.*
- (believable but incomplete) She should have said so earlier, so the group should not have to change anything now.
  - *It would have been easier earlier, but she is telling you now. Blaming her for the timing does not solve the problem.*

**2. Impact (the other side).** Two others say that the group should keep Saturday because it is the only day everyone else is free. What do you notice from their side?

- (careful move) They are worried about time and want the presentation to go well, which is a fair worry.
  - *Their concern is real too. Seeing it fairly makes it easier to find a plan that respects everyone.*
- (believable but incomplete) They are being selfish and only care about their own schedules and the grade.
  - *Calling them selfish ends the conversation. They are worried about the presentation, and that is understandable.*
- (believable but incomplete) Their schedule is the only one that counts, because most of the group can come on Saturday.
  - *Most of the group matters, but a plan that leaves one teammate out is a weaker plan for the whole group.*

**3. Consider.** What are some ways the group could adjust?

- (careful move) Look for a time that works for everyone, such as Friday or Sunday night, before anyone gives something up.
  - *Searching for a new time first treats both concerns as real, and often turns up an option nobody had tried.*
- (believable but incomplete) Keep Saturday, since most people can come, and let Amira catch up with the group later.
  - *It saves time for most people, but it leaves Amira to do the catching up and may leave her feeling like an afterthought.*
- (believable but incomplete) Let Amira join by message on Saturday and share her part in writing without being there.
  - *Joining by message is better than nothing, but she would miss the practice, and the group would miss her.*

**4. Choose.** What do you say or do now?

- (careful move) Propose a Friday or Sunday practice, and ask everyone what works before deciding.
  - *This keeps the group together and shows that every schedule matters. It also gives people a say.*
- (also good) Offer to practice her part with her separately, and share notes with the group afterward.
  - *Also a solid idea. It keeps the group's plan and still gives Amira real practice, though she misses the group time.*
- (unsafe) Go ahead on Saturday, and tell Amira that she will have to catch up on her own.
  - *This makes Amira pay the whole cost of the plan. It also risks a weaker presentation without her.*
- (ask a trusted adult) Ask your teacher whether the date can move, if no time works for everyone in the group.
  - *If no time works, a teacher may have room to help, and asking is a reasonable step.*

**5. Private reflection.** When has a plan that worked for you been hard for someone else?


## Story 2: A fast discussion and a teammate who waits for a pause

**1. Recognize (the first teammate's side).** Your group's discussions move quickly, and people often talk over each other. Kenji grew up where interrupting is considered rude, so he waits for a pause that never comes. He has said very little in three meetings. What do you notice from his side?

- (careful move) He may be waiting for his turn out of respect, and quiet does not mean he does not care.
  - *That is a generous and likely reading. It keeps you from deciding something about him that may not be true.*
- (believable but incomplete) He just does not care about the project, or he would have said something by now.
  - *That is a guess, and it may be wrong. Waiting politely can look like not caring when it is really good manners.*
- (believable but incomplete) He is shy, so it is his job to speak up, and the group should not have to change.
  - *He may be shy, but the group's way of talking is part of the problem. Expecting him to change alone is not fair.*

**2. Impact (the other side).** The group has started to say that Kenji is "not into it." What do you notice from the group's side?

- (careful move) They move fast to finish, and they may not realize that they are shutting someone out.
  - *Their intentions are probably good. Seeing that helps you ask for a change without blaming them.*
- (believable but incomplete) The group is doing nothing wrong, because fast, overlapping talk is simply how meetings work.
  - *Plenty of meetings run that way, and it works for some people. It does not work for everyone, and that matters here.*
- (believable but incomplete) Everyone should speak up if they want to be heard, so there is nothing for the group to fix here.
  - *Speaking up is easier for some people than for others. A group can make room for both kinds of people.*

**3. Consider.** What are some ways the group could adjust?

- (careful move) Agree on a simple way to take turns, such as going around the table, and invite written ideas.
  - *Turns and written ideas give quieter people a clear way in, and they often help everyone listen better.*
- (believable but incomplete) Ask Kenji to speak up more, and tell him that the group would like to hear his ideas.
  - *The invitation is kind, but it asks him to change how he behaves in a room that has not changed.*
- (believable but incomplete) Keep the meetings the same, and add a note at the end asking for any ideas by message.
  - *A message after the meeting helps a little, but the real conversation still happens without him.*

**4. Choose.** What do you say or do now?

- (careful move) Suggest that the group go around the table for each topic, and explain why it helps everyone.
  - *This gives everyone a turn, and it is easy to say yes to because it benefits the whole group.*
- (also good) Ask Kenji privately how he likes to share ideas, and offer to bring them to the group.
  - *Also a thoughtful choice. It respects how he likes to work, though the group still needs a better way to talk.*
- (unsafe) Tell the group that Kenji is not pulling his weight, and suggest that his part go to someone else.
  - *This judges him without asking, and it puts the blame on one person for something the whole group shaped.*
- (ask a trusted adult) Ask your teacher or advisor to help the group set up norms for how discussions will work.
  - *A teacher can help the whole group agree on a fair way to talk, which takes the pressure off any one person.*

**5. Private reflection.** What helps you speak up, and what helps you listen?


## Story 3: A dinner that some teammates cannot afford

**1. Recognize (the first teammate's side).** Your group is planning a celebration dinner at a restaurant that costs $45 each. Lucia quietly tells you that she cannot afford it this month and will skip it. What do you notice from her side?

- (careful move) She may feel embarrassed, and she told you because she trusts you to be kind about it.
  - *That is a gentle way to see it. Her trust is something to protect, not something to share.*
- (believable but incomplete) She should just find the money, since it is a small price for a nice night with friends.
  - *What is small for one person can be big for another. You cannot know what her month looks like.*
- (believable but incomplete) She is making a big deal out of nothing, since she could just come and order water.
  - *Ordering water still means sitting at a table where everyone else eats. It may not feel simple to her.*

**2. Impact (the other side).** Two others say that splitting the cost equally is only fair and that everyone can manage $45. What do you notice from their side?

- (careful move) They may assume that everyone has the same budget, and not realize that this leaves someone out.
  - *Most people do not mean to exclude anyone. They just have not thought about it yet.*
- (believable but incomplete) Splitting equally is always fair, so there is nothing more to think about here.
  - *Equal can feel fair, but it is not fair if one person cannot take part at all.*
- (believable but incomplete) They are being unkind on purpose, and they do not care about Lucia at all.
  - *That is a harsh guess. They are more likely to be thinking about their own budgets.*

**3. Consider.** What are some ways the group could adjust?

- (careful move) Look for a plan that costs less or that lets people choose, without asking Lucia to explain her money.
  - *A lower-cost plan helps everyone and spares Lucia from explaining. She gets to stay private.*
- (believable but incomplete) Ask Lucia to explain why she cannot pay, so that the group understands and can decide.
  - *Asking her to explain puts her on the spot, and her money is her own business.*
- (believable but incomplete) Have the group chip in for Lucia in front of everyone, so that she can come along.
  - *It is a kind impulse, but doing it in front of others can leave her feeling like a charity case.*

**4. Choose.** What do you say or do now?

- (careful move) Suggest a lower-cost option for everyone, such as a potluck or a cheaper restaurant.
  - *Everyone can join, nobody has to explain, and the evening still feels like a celebration.*
- (also good) Ask the group to set a spending limit before choosing a place, and offer a few options.
  - *Also a good approach. A limit set early keeps the plan open to everyone without singling anyone out.*
- (unsafe) Collect money from the group to cover Lucia, and announce it so that she knows it was handled.
  - *Announcing it draws attention to the thing she wanted kept private, even if you meant well.*
- (ask a trusted adult) Ask your advisor whether the celebration can have a set budget that works for the whole group.
  - *An advisor can help set a budget and keep the focus on the group, which takes pressure off any one person.*

**5. Private reflection.** How can a group plan something fun that does not depend on how much money people have?


## Story 4: A video with no captions

**1. Recognize (the first teammate's side).** Your group is planning a video presentation. Marcus is hard of hearing, and he says that without captions he cannot follow the videos the group plans to watch together for research. What do you notice from his side?

- (careful move) He wants to take part in the research on equal terms, not hear about it afterward.
  - *That is the heart of it. Being included at the same time is different from catching up later.*
- (believable but incomplete) He can catch up from a written transcript later, so there is no problem to solve.
  - *A transcript helps, but he would still miss the discussion while the group watches.*
- (believable but incomplete) He should ask the teacher for a special arrangement, since it is not the group's job.
  - *The teacher may help, but the group can solve this faster, and it is better to solve it together.*

**2. Impact (the other side).** Two teammates say that captions would take too much time. What do you notice from their side?

- (careful move) They are worried about time, and may not know how quick it is to turn on or add captions.
  - *Their worry is real, and the fix is often smaller than it seems. Sharing that can ease the concern.*
- (believable but incomplete) Captions are a favor for one person, so the group should not be asked to change.
  - *Captions help many people, such as anyone watching without sound. It is not just one person's need.*
- (believable but incomplete) The group should not have to change plans for one teammate when most people can hear fine.
  - *A small change that lets everyone take part is usually worth it, and it makes the group stronger.*

**3. Consider.** What are some ways the group could adjust?

- (careful move) Pick videos that already have captions, or turn on automatic captions and check them together.
  - *Many videos already have captions, and checking automatic ones takes only a few minutes.*
- (believable but incomplete) Skip the videos that Marcus cannot follow, and use only the ones that the group can all hear.
  - *That avoids the problem, but it limits the research, and Marcus may feel that he is the reason.*
- (believable but incomplete) Have Marcus watch the videos separately, and share his notes with the group afterward.
  - *It lets him see the videos, but he misses the shared conversation and works alone.*

**4. Choose.** What do you say or do now?

- (careful move) Pick videos with captions, and turn on captions in the video that the group makes.
  - *This includes Marcus now and makes the final video easier for anyone to follow.*
- (also good) Suggest that the group watch together with captions on, and share notes after each video.
  - *Also good. Everyone sees the same thing at the same time, and the notes help anyone who missed a detail.*
- (unsafe) Go ahead without captions, and tell Marcus that he can read the transcript later.
  - *This puts the whole cost on Marcus and makes him a spectator in his own project.*
- (ask a trusted adult) Ask your teacher about caption tools that the school provides, if you are not sure where to start.
  - *Schools often have tools and help for this, and a teacher can point you to them.*

**5. Private reflection.** What small change could make something easier for more people than the one it was made for?
